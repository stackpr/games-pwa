Somewhere Between

One player sees a hidden spot on a sliding scale and writes a clue. Everyone else places their own marker and scores their own guess.

Giving the clue

      How to play

      • The scale runs between two opposites — Cold to Hot, Trash to Treasure. A hidden target sits somewhere along it.
      • The clue-giver looks at the target, types one clue and passes the phone on. They are the only person who ever sees where the target is.
      • Everyone else guesses in turn, one at a time. Nobody sees anyone else's marker until the end, so nobody can just copy.
      • 4, 3 or 2 points depending on which band you land in, and nothing outside them.
      • The clue-giver scores everything the others scored, added together — so a clue that lands for one person and loses everybody else is a bad clue.

      Why there is no number

      • The marker has no percentage on it, on purpose. A number turns the argument into arithmetic — "sixty-two, not sixty-five" — when the interesting part is more that way, less that way.
